# Rate limits on login and signup (TAT-8)

Date: 2026-10-17
Refs: TAT-8 (Rate-limit login, signup and password reset): "Use `@adonisjs/limiter` with a Postgres store (no Redis in V1). Limit per IP and per email. Return a friendly error." Stacked on TAT-82 (guest pages clear page history). Password reset itself is TAT-18. Designed unattended: every open question is decided in the Rulings below.

## Goal

Nobody can guess passwords at scale from one address or against one account, or mass-create accounts from one address. A refused visitor sees "Too many attempts. Try again in N minutes." on the form they used. The refusal never reveals whether an email has an account, and one attacker's address alone can never lock someone else out. Counters live in Postgres and hold no email or IP address in clear.

No API, SDK, OpenAPI, page or route-name change. One migration (`rate_limits`). No new environment variable.

## What the installed packages do

Read from `@adonisjs/limiter` 3.0.1 (latest, for `@adonisjs/core` 7) and `rate-limiter-flexible` 9.1.1, not from memory:

- `limiter.use({ requests, duration })` returns a `Limiter`, cached per options. `consume(key)` adds a point with one atomic upsert (`INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE … RETURNING`) and throws `errors.E_TOO_MANY_REQUESTS` once the count passes `requests`. `error.response.availableIn` is the seconds left in the window. Windows are fixed: a key's window starts at its first point. `use()` parses the durations **in the object it is given** (it mutates it).
- `limiter.penalize(key, fn)` reads the count, runs `fn`, then increments. Parallel requests all pass the read.
- `limiter.define(name, builder)` returns route middleware that consumes one point on **every** request, with key `${name}_${usingKey}`.
- `ThrottleException.handle`: HTML answers a plain-text 429; JSON gets 429 `{ errors: [{ message, retryAfter }] }` with `Retry-After` and `X-RateLimit-*` headers. Under Inertia the plain-text 429 would show as a raw-response modal.
- The database store takes `db.connection(name).getWriteClient()` once, when the limiter is first created. Each query then runs on its own pooled connection, outside any Lucid transaction. It never creates its table (`tableCreated: true`), inserts positional `VALUES (key, points, expire)` with `expire` in epoch ms, and deletes rows that expired over an hour ago every 5 minutes (`clearExpiredByTimeout`). `clear()` runs `TRUNCATE`.
- `request.ip()` (`@adonisjs/http-server` 9.3.1) is `proxy-addr` with `trustProxy` defaulting to `proxyAddr.compile("loopback")`. `config/app.ts` doesn't set it.

## Limits

| Limit | Counts | Max | Window |
|---|---|---|---|
| Login, per client address | attempts that don't sign in (wrong password, unknown email, refused) | 10 | 15 min |
| Login, per email (any address) | failed attempts | 25 | 15 min |
| Signup, per client address | every request | 10 | 1 hour |

**A lockout** refuses further attempts with that address or email until its window ends: at most 15 minutes (login) or 1 hour (signup) after the first counted attempt. The right password is refused too, unchecked. Nothing is stored on the user and no email is sent. A sign-in clears the email's failures and gives the address its attempt back.

## Rulings

1. **Login is limited per address and per email; signup per address only. Password reset is not built here.** TAT-18 builds reset. It adds its limits to `AUTH_LIMITS` and a named middleware to `start/limiter.ts`. The exception handler already gives any limit the friendly message (Rules for other tickets). Rejected: a reset limiter now, which has no route to guard; and a per-email signup limit, since an email can register once and the unique check already answers for it.
2. **Login counts failures, and counts them before checking.** Points are taken before `verify` runs and refunded on success. Why: a studio's staff share one address and sign in often, and parallel guesses must not all pass a read-then-count check. Rejected: `penalize()`, which races, and on any success would clear an address too, so an attacker could reset their address by signing into their own account; and counting every login request per address, which blocks a busy front desk.
3. **Numbers and windows as in Limits: fixed windows, no extra block (`blockFor`).** 25 is more than twice 10 over the same window. One address fits at most two of its own windows into one email window, so it adds at most 20 failures and can never lock an account alone. A unit test pins that relation. Accepted risk: three or more addresses can keep an account locked by sending 25 failures every 15 minutes. TAT-18 can let a completed reset clear the count; CAPTCHA and 2FA are out of V1. Rejected: `blockFor` penalties, which lengthen a victim's lockout; keying the email on email+IP (Laravel's way), which puts no cap on guessing from many addresses; a small global email limit, which lets one address lock any account.
4. **No account enumeration.** The email count takes any submitted email, registered or not, before any lookup. The refusal message is the same for both limits, and `verifyCredentials` already hashes for unknown emails. Signup's "email already taken" error is an existing enumeration channel. The signup limit caps it at 10 tries per address per hour; removing it belongs to TAT-18's verification flow.
5. **Keys are keyed digests.** `login_email_<digest(trimmed, lower-cased email)>`, `login_ip_<digest(clientAddress(ip))>`, `signup_<digest(clientAddress(ip))>`. Why: no PII in clear; a fixed 43-character digest, where a 254-character email plus prefix would overflow `varchar(255)`; case variants share a count; and an IPv6 /64 is one subscriber, so rotating within it buys nothing. Rotating `APP_KEY` resets all counts, which is acceptable. Rejected: raw keys; plain SHA-256, which a guess list reverses, and the IPv4 space is tiny.
6. **The store is Postgres `rate_limits` as the app role, outside the request transaction.** rate-limiter-flexible's SQL fixes the table's shape, so it departs from the uuid/timestamptz conventions on purpose. Not tenant-owned: no RLS, with a reasoned `EXEMPT` entry. `setup_roles.sql`'s default privileges give the app role SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE and DELETE. It gets no TRUNCATE, so app code never calls `limiter.clear()` on it. Counts commit on their own connection, so a failed login still counts after the exception handler rolls the request back. Installed by hand: `node ace add` would write a `LIMITER_STORE` variable into `.env` and add a sample global throttle. Rejected: Redis (the ticket); the memory store in production (per process, lost on restart).
7. **The friendly error behaves like a wrong password.** An HTML/Inertia request gets a 302 back to the form, with the error toast "Too many attempts. Try again in N minutes." (whole minutes, rounded up, at least 1). Old input is flashed except passwords. A JSON request keeps the package's 429 with `Retry-After`. The exception handler does this once for every `E_TOO_MANY_REQUESTS`. No page changes. Rejected: a 429 page, which Inertia shows as a raw-response modal; an inline field error, since login shows credential errors as a toast today and an email-field error would point at the email.
8. **Proxies keep the framework default (`trustProxy` = loopback).** `X-Forwarded-For` counts only when the direct peer is loopback (a proxy on the same host), so a client can't spoof its address. Behind a proxy elsewhere (a platform load balancer), every visitor would share the proxy's address. A production checklist item says to set `trustProxy` to that proxy's addresses, never `true`. A unit test pins the default. Rejected: `trustProxy: true`, which is spoofable; an env var now, with no deploy target yet.
9. **Tests use the memory store, emptied before every test; one spec covers Postgres.** `app.inTest` picks memory. `tests/bootstrap.ts` taps every functional/browser group and test with `limiter.clear(["memory"])`, so no test inherits another's counts. Only `["memory"]`: a plain `clear()` would also truncate Postgres once the store spec has created a database limiter. Tests set the address with `X-Forwarded-For`, which loopback peers may send. `rate_limit_store.spec.ts` runs the Postgres store with no global transaction: the store keeps the client it was first created with, and inside `wrapInGlobalTransaction` that would be one test's transaction. The rest of the suite stays green because it signs in with `loginAs` (no `POST /login`); only 4 existing tests post to `/login` or `/signup`, with correct passwords. Rejected: a `LIMITER_STORE` env var, a new variable for everyone's `.env` that could put production on memory by mistake; the database store in every test, which has the capture problem and costs a query per test.

## Tests

- Unit `tests/unit/services/rate_limits.spec.ts`: `clientAddress` (IPv4 and IPv4-mapped forms, one /64 for several spellings, another /64, a zone id, a non-address); `digest` (43 base64url characters, stable, keyed, unlike plain SHA-256, fixed length for a 254-character input); `tryAgainMessage` (0 → 1 minute, 61 → 2 minutes, 3600 → 60 minutes); `http.trustProxy` trusts `127.0.0.1` and `::1`, not public or private LAN peers.
- Unit `tests/unit/modules/identity/auth_limits.spec.ts`: the email limit is above twice the address limit over the same window, and the keys are digests (no email or address inside, case and spaces ignored, IPv4-mapped = IPv4, one key per IPv6 /64).
- Functional `tests/functional/auth/rate_limit_store.spec.ts`: as the app role, the Postgres store counts, refuses the third request with `E_TOO_MANY_REQUESTS`, writes one row to `rate_limits`, reports `availableIn` within the window, and deletes the key.
- Functional `tests/functional/auth/rate_limits.spec.ts`:
  - 10 failures from one address: the 11th, even with the right password, goes back to `/login` with the 15-minute message; a fake address put in front of the real one in `X-Forwarded-For` doesn't help; as JSON it is a 429 with `Retry-After`; another address still signs in.
  - 15 parallel wrong passwords from one address: exactly 10 are checked, 5 are refused.
  - 25 failures for one email from three addresses (case varied): the 26th from a fourth address is refused even with the right password; another account signs in from that address.
  - A sign-in after 3 failures leaves the address at 3 and clears the email.
  - An unknown email is counted like a registered one.
  - Counts don't carry over to the next test.
  - 10 signup requests (invalid bodies) from one address: the 11th, valid, goes back to `/signup` with the 60-minute message and creates no user; another address signs up.
- Not automated: a production proxy setup, and the toast itself (browser pass).

## Browser pass (the user)

1. With `pnpm dev`, enter a wrong password 10 times on `/login`, then the right one: the toast says "Too many attempts. Try again in 15 minutes." and you stay on the form.
2. As the owner, `select key, points from rate_limits`: keys look like `rlflx:login_ip_…` and `rlflx:login_email_…` digests, with no email or IP. `delete from rate_limits` unlocks.

## Rules for other tickets

- **TAT-18 (password reset, verification mail):** reset pages go in the `guest` group (TAT-82). Add per-address and per-email reset limits to `AUTH_LIMITS`, as `limiter.define` middleware in `start/limiter.ts` that count every request. A reset request must answer the same for unknown emails, so it can't count only failures. Key the email with `digest`. A completed reset may clear the email's login failures (`limiter.use({ ...AUTH_LIMITS.loginPerEmail }).delete(loginEmailKey(email))`). Verification and reset mail sends need their own per-email limit.
- **API rate limits (per IP and tenant, docs/architecture.md):** reuse the store, `digest` and `clientAddress`. Align the JSON 429 with the OpenAPI `RateLimited` envelope (`{ error: { code: "rate_limited", message } }`) there.
- Always pass `limiter.use()` a copy of a shared limits object.

## Not in this ticket

Password reset and email verification (TAT-18); API and intake rate limits; CAPTCHA, 2FA and lockout notification emails; `trustProxy` for a specific host; i18n of the message.

# Reviews and Fresh ink (client posts)

Date: 2026-11-12
Status: **design, agreed with the product owner; not built.** Built after work photo angles and video angles, whose media pipeline it reuses.
Builds on: completed projects and session outcomes (TAT-85), client records (`clients`), the deposit page's token links (TAT-65b: a single-use credential in a link, no account), message templates and chat links (TAT-52), the image pipeline (`ImageService`, TAT-112/116), work photo angles and the video pipeline (this round), Page look sections (TAT-126), the storefront themes, and Discover (D02–D10: the `discovery` views, publication controls, takedowns, SEO).
Reverses: `docs/roadmap.md` lists "loyalty and reviews" and "customer accounts" under *Later, maybe never*. The product owner has decided to build reviews now; customer accounts stay out (ruling 2). The roadmap and requirements are updated in the first slice.

## Goal

A client who got tattooed at a studio can say how it went (a rating and a review) and show the healed tattoo (photos and short videos, "Fresh ink"), tagged with the studio and the artist who made it. The studio shows them on its own site; Discover shows them on the studio's and the artist's profiles and in a global Fresh ink feed. Every review and every post comes from a real client of that studio, so Discover's rule holds: no fake listings, reviews, popularity or statistics.

## Rulings

1. **Only verified clients post, through the studio's link.** A review and a post are written through a private link the studio sends after a completed project (ruling 3), never from an open form. The studio and the artist are the project's, so a tag can never be false and nobody can post about a studio they never visited. Why: no accounts exist, and an open form would invite fake praise and attacks from competitors that no moderation keeps up with. Rejected: anyone tags any studio (needs client accounts, identity checks and heavy moderation); the studio types a review in on the client's behalf (unverifiable).
2. **No client accounts.** The link is the credential, as the deposit link is (TAT-65b): a random token, stored as a digest, scoped to one project, sent by the studio through the client's chat app or email. The same link lets the client edit or delete what they posted, for as long as it lives (ruling 4). Why: accounts are a large separate product (sign-up, recovery, privacy deletion) and the roadmap still keeps them out. Rejected: email magic links per client (an account in all but name).
3. **The studio asks, once the project is done.** "Ask for a review" appears on a project whose tattoo work is finished (a completed tattoo session, TAT-85; the project `in_progress`, `completed` or `healing_check`), owners and the project's linked artist. It makes the link and opens the existing chat or email choice with a new message template ("How did it heal? Tell us and show it off: <link>"). One link per project; asking again shows the same link (or a new one if the old was revoked). Why: the healed result is what clients want to show, and the studio already talks to them there. Rejected: asking automatically by email (studios here talk on WhatsApp and Viber, and an unprompted email reads as spam).
4. **The link lives a year and can be revoked.** It opens the client's page for 12 months from when it is made; the studio can revoke it from the project. A revoked or expired link shows a plain "This link no longer works" with the studio's contact links, and never what was posted. What was posted stays published after the link expires; the client asks the studio (or TattooDrip, ruling 12) to take it down after that. Why: healing takes weeks to months; a credential should not live forever.
5. **One review and one Fresh ink post per project.** The review: a rating of 1 to 5 (whole stars) and a text of 20 to 2,000 characters, plain text. The post: 1 to 8 photos or videos (the angles model: a lead and up to 7 more; videos by the video pipeline's rules: 60 s, 100 MB, audio stripped, never the lead), a caption of up to 280 characters, up to three styles from the studio's list (preselected from the project's), and optionally where it is on the body (the inquiry's placement words). Either can exist without the other. Why: one honest account per tattoo; several reviews from one project would inflate a rating.
6. **Reviews publish when the client submits; studios reply and report, never hide.** A submitted review is public at once on every surface the client allowed (ruling 8). The studio can post one public reply (up to 1,000 characters, editable) and can report the review to TattooDrip for abuse (hate, threats, personal data, off-topic, not a client). A reported review stays up until TattooDrip decides (ruling 12). Why: if a studio could hide its bad reviews, every rating would mean nothing, and Discover's no-fake-signals rule would be broken in spirit. Rejected: studio approval of reviews; hiding below a rating.
7. **Fresh ink posts need the studio's approval.** A post (photos and videos of the client's body, on the studio's own brand) waits in the studio's Reviews page as "Waiting for you" until the studio approves or declines it; declining tells the client nothing beyond "not shown by the studio" on their page, and they can still keep their review. An approved post can be unpublished by the studio later (it is their page) but not edited. Why: a studio must not be forced to show a photo of work it does not stand behind or of poor quality; this is different from the honesty of a written review. Rejected: posts publish at once (a studio page could show anything); studios editing captions.
8. **The client chooses where it shows.** Two consents on the client's page, each a separate unticked checkbox with plain words: "Show on <studio>'s page" and "Show on TattooDrip Discover" (the second only while the studio is listed on Discover; ruling 11). A review or post with neither is kept for the studio only (it still counts nowhere public). The client can change either later through the link. Why: these are photos of a person's body and their words; consent is per surface and withdrawable. Rejected: one consent for both; consent by default.
9. **Names: first name and initial.** Public surfaces show the client's first name and the initial of their last name ("Sita R."), from the client record, never their phone, email or full name. The client can change the shown name on their page (1 to 40 characters, any script), within the same rule of not being someone else's name (a report reason). Why: enough to feel real, little enough to protect them. Rejected: full names; anonymous reviews (less trustworthy, and the verification already proves a real client).
10. **Ratings count only from three reviews.** A studio's or artist's average and count are shown on Discover only once it has at least 3 published reviews with Discover consent; below that, the reviews are listed without an average. The studio's own page shows the reviews; its average follows the same rule. The average is to one decimal ("4.7 from 12 reviews"); the order is newest first, never "best first". Google's review rich results: `AggregateRating` and `Review` JSON-LD only on Discover's profiles (a third party about the business), only from 3 reviews, never on the studio's own site (self-serving reviews are not eligible). Why: one or two reviews make a misleading average; Discover's blueprint forbids invented signals.
11. **Discover shows only what both the studio and the client allowed.** A review or post is on Discover only if the studio is listed (D04's consent, and not taken down), the artist is shown on Discover, and the client ticked Discover consent. A studio unlisting from Discover takes every review and post off Discover at once (the views' rule), and they return if it lists again while the client's consent stands.
12. **TattooDrip can take anything down.** An operator command (like `discover:suspend`, D04) takes a review, a reply or a post off every public surface with a note, and restores it. Reports from studios land in an operator list. The client's link page says when something was taken down and why, in a fixed sentence per reason. Terms for client content (a licence to TattooDrip and the studio to show it, the client's right to delete it) are drafted by Claude and marked "Draft: not legally reviewed" until the product owner has them reviewed; nothing is public before that (the review page works behind a flag). Why: the platform is responsible for what Discover shows, and studios report abuse they may not remove.

## Data (all tenant-owned: row-level security, `enableTenantRls`, isolation tests)

- `review_links`: `id`, `tenant_id`, `project_id` (unique per live link), `token_hash`, `created_by`, `created_at`, `expires_at`, `revoked_at`. Not soft-deleted (a credential, as `deposit_links`).
- `client_reviews`: `id`, `tenant_id`, `project_id` (unique), `client_id`, `artist_id`, `rating` (check 1..5), `body`, `shown_name`, `consent_studio_page`, `consent_discover`, `reply`, `replied_at`, `reported_at`, `report_reason`, `taken_down_at`, `takedown_note`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `deleted_at` (the client's delete is a soft delete for the studio's record; nothing public reads it).
- `client_posts`: like reviews without rating/body/reply, plus `caption`, `styles` (up to three slugs, as work photos), `placement`, `status` (`waiting`, `approved`, `declined`, `unpublished`), `decided_at`, `decided_by`; its media in `client_post_media` (`post_id`, `kind` photo or video, the image or video keys, `width`, `height`, `alt`, `position` 0..7 with the deferrable unique, video `duration_ms`, `poster_key`, `processing_state`), new public media purposes `client_post_photo` and `client_post_video`.
- Discover: new views `discovery.reviews` and `discovery.fresh_ink` (owned by `tattoo_drip_discovery`, column grants, the restrictive policies of D02), only rows with Discover consent, published or approved, not taken down, of listed studios and shown artists; `docs/discover/database-security.md` and `tests/functional/rls/discovery.spec.ts` updated in the same change.

## Surfaces

- **The client's page** (storefront, `/review/<token>`, unthemed like `/deposit`, `noindex`, `private, no-store`, the token kept in the fragment as the deposit link's is, so it never reaches a log): the project's studio, artist and date; the rating and review; Fresh ink upload with previews (the dashboard's preview conventions); the two consents; the shown name; then edit and delete. Turnstile on submit (TAT-55), rate limits per link.
- **Dashboard:** "Ask for a review" on the project page; a **Reviews** page (owners; artists see their own): Fresh ink waiting for approval (approve, decline), published reviews (reply, report), with counts in the nav like the Inbox.
- **Studio site:** two Page look sections, `reviews` and `fresh_ink` (off by default; on once there is something to show), in every theme; each theme draws them in its own idiom. A tattoo's Fresh ink opens in the existing viewer with its angles.
- **Discover:** reviews and Fresh ink on studio and artist profiles; a global `/fresh-ink` feed of consented posts, newest first, filterable by style (ruling 5) and city, each linking to the studio's profile and booking.

## Build order

1. **Data, links and the client page**: migrations, services, the token link (digest, expiry, revoke), the `/review/<token>` page with upload, consents and edit/delete; roadmap, requirements and terms drafts. Behind a feature flag until the terms are reviewed.
2. **Dashboard**: Ask for a review, the message template, the Reviews page (approve, decline, reply, report), operator commands and the report list.
3. **Studio site**: the `reviews` and `fresh_ink` sections in every theme; the public API (`listReviews`, `listFreshInk`, contract first, SDK, hooks).
4. **Discover**: the views, the Discover API, profile sections, the global feed, JSON-LD from 3 reviews.

Each slice: review with Fable at high effort before its PR, isolation and cross-tenant tests for every new route and query, browser checks at 375 and 1440, and docs updated with the code.

## Not here (carry-forwards)

- Client accounts, following studios, likes, comments on posts, sharing to Instagram.
- Reviews of a studio by someone who was not tattooed there (consultation-only visits).
- Ratings by category (cleanliness, communication).
- Translating reviews.
